Terms of Use and Data Privacy Notice

Version 1.0 · Effective September 2026

1. What this system is

The Scholarship Records Management System (SRMS) is the record system of the Scholarship and Development Services Office (SDSO) of Biliran Province State University. It registers scholarship applicants, evaluates and ranks them against each programme’s criteria, records awards and renewals, and reports scholars to the agencies that fund them.

An account here is a working record, not an award. Registering creates no entitlement to any scholarship, and nothing entered on this form decides a grant on its own.

2. Who may register

Accounts are for students currently enrolled at BiPSU and for BiPSU personnel applying to the BiPSU Employee Scholarship. One account per person. Registering on behalf of somebody else, or holding more than one account, is grounds for rejecting every account involved.

A self-registered account cannot sign in until the SDSO has verified it against university records, and the email address given must be confirmed before it will receive anything the office sends — including its own decision.

3. Truthfulness of what you declare

You confirm that every answer, figure and uploaded document is true, complete, and yours to submit. Much of what this form asks for — family income, Listahanan and 4Ps status, a disability, a scholarship already held — is verified by the office against its own records and the issuing agency’s before it counts for anything.

A declaration found to be false may have the account rejected, an award withdrawn, and the matter referred to the university under its student or personnel discipline rules. Where an agency outside BiPSU funds the grant, that agency’s own sanctions apply as well.

Tell the SDSO when something on the record changes — your enrolment status, your contact details, a scholarship newly held or lost. A record that was right on the day it was filed and is wrong afterwards is what causes a scholar to be dropped from a payroll.

4. Your account

You are responsible for what is done through your account. Keep the password to yourself, and tell the SDSO at once if you think somebody else has it. A submission made from an account is treated as made by the person it belongs to.

5. Personal data this system collects

Registering gives the university your identity and contact details, your enrolment and academic record, your educational background, and the socio-economic answers the programmes select on — household income and size, Listahanan and 4Ps membership, solo-parent and indigenous-group status, and whether you are a person with disability.

Some of that is sensitive personal information under the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173). It is collected because the programmes themselves are defined in those terms: a grant reserved for indigent households cannot be awarded without asking about the household. A document uploaded as proof is held on the same footing as the answer it proves.

6. What it is used for

Your record is processed to verify your account, to check you against the eligibility rules of the programmes you apply to or are matched with, to rank applicants where a programme has more of them than slots, to record and renew awards, and to produce the masterlists and reports the university and its funding partners require.

Statistics drawn from these records — counts by programme, school, course and term — are used for planning and reporting. Those are aggregates and name nobody.

7. Who else sees it

Inside BiPSU, your record is seen by SDSO staff and by the university officials who decide and sign off on scholarships.

Outside BiPSU, the record of a scholar is shared with the agency funding that scholarship — CHED, UniFAST, DOST, GSIS, a local government or a private partner, depending on the programme — because the agency requires it to release the grant and to audit it afterwards. A partner office signing in here sees only the scholars of the programmes it funds, and only the columns the SDSO has released to it.

Your record is not sold, and it is not given to anyone else except where a law, a subpoena, or a lawful order of a government body requires it.

8. How long it is kept

Records are kept for as long as you hold an account and a scholarship relationship with the university, and afterwards for as long as the university’s retention rules and the funding agencies’ audit requirements demand. Archived records stay available to the SDSO for verification and reporting, and are disposed of under those rules.

9. Your rights over your data

The Data Privacy Act of 2012 gives you the right to be informed how your data is processed, to have access to it, to correct anything inaccurate, to object to processing, to have your data erased or blocked in the circumstances the law provides, to be compensated for damage caused by its misuse, and to complain to the National Privacy Commission.

Much of your record you can correct yourself under My Profile. For anything locked to the office — the fields it has already verified — or to exercise any of the other rights above, write to the SDSO.

Withdrawing consent is your right, but it ends the processing a scholarship depends on: an application cannot be evaluated, and an award cannot be maintained or reported to its funder, without the record it is made from.

10. Keeping it safe

Access is restricted by role, every account is verified before it can sign in, uploaded documents are served only to those entitled to see them, and actions taken on records are logged. No system is perfectly secure and we do not claim otherwise — but a breach affecting your data will be handled as the Data Privacy Act requires, including notice to you and to the National Privacy Commission where the law calls for it.

11. Changes to this notice

These terms carry a version, and the version you agreed to is recorded on your account with the date you agreed to it. If the office revises them materially, you will be asked to read and agree to the new version.

12. Contact

Scholarship and Development Services Office, Biliran Province State University, Naval, Biliran. Reach the office through the contact details published on the university website, or at the SDSO itself, for anything concerning this notice or your record.
